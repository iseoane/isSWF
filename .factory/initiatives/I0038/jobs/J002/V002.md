The instruction supplies neither the expected-revision source nor the post-verification manifest extraction needed to obtain a version. Treating the result as metadata fails; following the stated missing/unavailable-input rules yields unknown.

It does not turn that source revision into a complete release. `docs/source-activation.md` explicitly describes source-checkout compatibility resources, not an installed runtime, and limits the receipt to integrity rather than authorization.

**Impact:** the advertised Pi alternative cannot execute the specified identity comparison as written. Inferring that any valid source-activation receipt establishes release identity would reintroduce the source/release conflation F001 required removing.

**Recommendation:** reconsider the supported provenance route before further implementation. Do not close F001 merely by reading a manifest after `verifyPackage`; that repairs extraction, not release classification. Any eventual route must distinguish source-resource integrity from full-release identity and specify the actual trusted inputs and returned metadata.
