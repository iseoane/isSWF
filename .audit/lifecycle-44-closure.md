## Recovery and ownership boundary

The host alone owns process metadata. The task owns source changes and any A001 artifact. The coordinator owns product acceptance. Nonzero exit can publish failure without A001. Same-ID launch remains blocked in running, terminal and unknown states. Distinct IDs derive distinct workspaces, not a general checkout lock or multiproject scheduler.

Read and reconcile are read-only evidence inspection. They do not resume execution, signal discovered PIDs, release claims, repair records or retry effects. A killed owner with potentially live children remains unknown and partial files survive. The verifier observes its explicitly owned fixture child cease naturally. This does not implement arbitrary crash recovery or automatic task resumption. A new attempt needs explicit trusted invocation and a distinct ID.

At a deadline, the owner signals its owned task group and uses bounded capture grace. Escaped descendants can remain uncertain. An incomplete capture marker is not proof all descendants died. There is no external watchdog, hostile-task sandbox, exhaustive filesystem authority control, power-loss guarantee or exactly-once effect guarantee.
