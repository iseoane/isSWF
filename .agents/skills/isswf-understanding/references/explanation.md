# Explain a topic or system

Identify what the person needs to understand next. Use their question to place the depth: a definition, a mechanism, a decision, or a walkthrough. Skip prerequisites they already demonstrate and explain only the missing ones.

For a mechanism, show the trigger, relevant steps, changes to state or data, and resulting behavior. Use one concrete example to connect those parts. Include exceptions when they change what the person should expect.

For a decision, distinguish the recorded motivation from a plausible advantage of the current design. Explain supported constraints and trade-offs. If the actual motivation is unknown, say so; a reasonable design justification is not historical evidence.

For a code walkthrough, trace the behavior that answers the question rather than narrating every line. Name only the files and symbols needed to navigate that behavior. A reader should understand the responsibilities and their interaction, not just possess a list of paths.

For a general concept, define it plainly, show what problem it addresses, and give an example at the person's level. Use an analogy only when useful and state where it stops matching the real mechanism.

Check the explanation against the request:

- Can the person identify what the thing is or what the answer means?
- Is the essential mechanism or reasoning visible without unexplained jargon?
- Does the example preserve the real constraints?
- Are evidence, inference, and uncertainty distinguishable?
- Does the response contain the requested depth without unrelated material?

These are checks on the explanation, not proof of the person's understanding. In conversation, use their follow-up to decide what needs a different treatment.
