# Help the person understand

Use this skill for any subject or project. Follow the person's language and applicable project conventions. The name identifies a utility, not a requirement to activate factory workflows.

## 1. Select the approach

Use the request and conversation to identify the person's goal and demonstrated knowledge. Do not assume expertise from their job title or lack of expertise from a basic question.

| Request | Approach |
| --- | --- |
| Restate a previous answer, remove jargon, or make it clearer | Reformulate the identified explanation in everyday language. Preserve its meaning, uncertainty, and important limits. Define necessary technical terms rather than removing them. |
| Explain a concept, show how something works, or explain a decision | Read [explanation](references/explanation.md). Build a useful mental model grounded in the relevant evidence. |
| Learn to do something, practise, or develop a skill | Read [learning](references/learning.md). Work toward a concrete application through manageable practice and feedback. |

Combine approaches only when the request calls for them. A request to explain something is not automatically a request for a course or an exercise. When the goal is clear, proceed without an intake interview. If a missing topic, referent, or conflicting goal would materially change the response, ask a focused question first.

This step ends when the topic and intended outcome are clear, or with the necessary clarification question.

## 2. Establish enough grounding

For a concrete system, inspect the relevant code, contracts, or supplied material before asserting how it behaves. For external, changing, disputed, or consequential claims, consult appropriate reliable sources when available. Keep investigation proportional to the question and current access.

Separate observed behavior, documented reasons, inference, and unknowns. A mechanism that explains current behavior does not establish why its author chose it. Preserve this distinction in plain-language explanations. Label hypothetical examples and the limits of any analogy.

For a simple reformulation, use the identified answer without reopening unrelated research. If it contains an unsupported or incorrect claim, flag or correct it rather than making it sound more certain.

This step ends when the response has adequate support and necessary uncertainties are identified. Missing access is a limit, not permission to invent evidence.

## 3. Explain at the useful depth

Start with a direct answer or plain definition, then connect it to the person's situation. Give the smallest complete explanation that addresses the request. Respect an explicit request for depth or a complete one-shot lesson rather than withholding needed detail across turns.

Define unfamiliar terms when they first matter and use consistent names. Preserve real symbols when they help the person connect an explanation to the system. Explain the mechanism instead of listing functions or replacing it with a metaphor.

Choose representations by the obstacle to understanding:

| Obstacle | Useful representation |
| --- | --- |
| Order or interaction | A short sequence or flow. |
| Ownership or structure | A shallow responsibility tree or map. |
| Alternatives or trade-offs | A focused comparison table. |
| What changed | A before-and-after view or diff. |
| Applying a rule | A worked example. |

Use prose alone when sufficient. Put visuals beside the ideas they explain. Build a complex picture incrementally when that helps, without imposing a diagram count or format. Create or open files only when requested or otherwise authorized; ordinary explanations stay in the conversation.

## 4. Adapt and finish

If the person remains confused, change the explanation: choose a different example, expose a missing prerequisite, reduce the scope, or change representation. Avoid repeating the same account with cosmetic wording changes. Ask which part is unclear only when the conversation does not reveal the obstacle.

Use exercises and feedback for an actual learning request, respecting a preference to skip them. Do not quiz someone just to close an explanation. Silence, agreement, or a fluent explanation does not demonstrate mastery.

Before responding, check that the answer addresses the actual question, fits the known level, preserves necessary qualifications, and does not present illustrative material as observed behavior. Deliver the explanation or lesson itself, not a report about the teaching process. Stop when the requested outcome is addressed; leave unanswered questions or unavailable evidence explicit.

Learning progress, saved materials, and later review are optional. Do not create records, modify a project, schedule reminders, or contact communities merely because the person asked to understand something. Use the project's writing workflow when a durable document is requested, without making another skill a prerequisite for a conversational answer.
