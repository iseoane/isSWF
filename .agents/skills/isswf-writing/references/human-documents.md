# Documents for humans

## Choose the reader's task

Identify what the reader needs to achieve and what they already know. Choose the dominant purpose:

| Need | Approach |
| --- | --- |
| Learn through practice | Give a guided exercise with visible results and enough support to succeed. |
| Complete a known task | Give prerequisites, ordered actions, relevant branches, and expected results. |
| Look up facts | Organize contracts, options, limits, and errors to match the system being described. |
| Understand a topic or decision | Explain mechanisms, reasons, constraints, and meaningful alternatives. |

Keep a clear primary purpose. Separate substantial digressions into linked material when that helps the reader; a short necessary explanation does not require a new document.

## Make the document usable

Lead with the result or question the document addresses. Supply context before unfamiliar instructions. Assume only the knowledge appropriate to the intended reader.

Use headings that help readers find their task or question. In procedures, identify the starting point and explain what successful steps produce. Put recovery guidance beside the failure it addresses when relevant.

Choose the smallest useful representation. Use a table for choices, a shallow tree for responsibilities, a diagram for relationships, or a diff for a change. Keep labels factual and omit decorative views.

Examples must match supported behavior. Label illustrative or hypothetical examples as such. Keep credentials and private data out of examples. Preserve valid syntax and the project's formatting conventions.

## Check from the reader's perspective

- Can the reader identify whether this document addresses their need?
- Are prerequisites and unfamiliar terms explained or linked at the point of need?
- Can the reader follow the procedure or locate the required fact without reconstructing missing context?
- Are outcomes, limitations, and important failure conditions explicit?
- Does each example or visual clarify something the text alone makes harder to understand?
- Are required translations equivalent in meaning, examples, and navigation under the project's rules?
